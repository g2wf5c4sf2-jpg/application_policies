Privacy policy — Response Card

Response Card displays a contact card issued to you by the organization that sent you an enrolment link. It has no accounts, no sign-up, and no advertising. Response Card, and its text message program, are operated by SpunkyChicken.

This policy covers two things: the Response Card app on your phone, and the Response Card text messages (SMS and MMS) that deliver your card. The Response Card SMS terms and conditions cover the text messages in more detail.

Android: what the app stores on your device

  • The contact card you were issued, encrypted. It is unlocked with your fingerprint, face or device PIN, and cannot be read without one.
  • A login value, encrypted separately, shown only after you confirm again.
  • A credential that lets the app ask for updates to your card. This one is not behind your fingerprint, because the app has to use it when an update arrives and you are not there. It is unreadable while your phone is locked.

All three are encrypted with keys held in your phone's secure hardware. They cannot be copied off the device, and none of them can be read while the screen is locked.

This app needs a screen lock to work. Without one there is nowhere on the phone to keep a card safely, and it will say so rather than store it anyway.

Android: what the app sends

  • A notification token from Google, so your card can be told when it changes.
  • The credential above, when it asks for an update.

Both go only to the service operated by the organization that issued your card. Transmission uses HTTPS.

What the service records

When your phone asks for an update, the service notes the address it connected from, along with what was asked for and when. Those records are deleted after ninety days.

They exist so that a card being used on more phones than it was issued to can be noticed. They are not used to work out where you are, they are not combined with anything else, and they go nowhere else.

Apple iPhone and iPad

On iPhone, your card is an Apple Wallet pass. There is no app to install. The card is a pass signed by the organization that issued it, and Apple Wallet keeps it on your iPhone. It is protected by your iPhone's own security: its passcode, Face ID or Touch ID. If your Wallet passes sync through iCloud, Apple keeps the pass on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, under Apple's own privacy policy.

When you add the pass, Wallet registers it with the service operated by the organization that issued your card, so the card can be kept up to date. Wallet sends:

  • An identifier Wallet makes up for your device, and a push token from Apple Push Notification service (APNs).
  • The card's serial number and its credential, when it asks for an update.
  • Wallet's own error messages about the pass, if something goes wrong fetching it.

When your card changes, the service sends a push through APNs to that token. For a Wallet pass the push carries nothing: no card contents, no name, no notice. It only tells Wallet to fetch the new card from the service over HTTPS. Wallet may then show the short headline of a notice from the organization on your lock screen, the same line described under Notifications below.

Removing the pass from Wallet tells the service to stop sending updates to that device. The service records each request the same way as for any other phone (see What the service records). It also keeps the time and country of the card's first device registration with the card's record, so a card used on more devices than it was issued to can be noticed.

On iPad, your card is held in the Response Card app. An iPad cannot hold a Wallet pass. The app keeps your card encrypted with a key in the iPad's Secure Enclave. It opens only with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and it cannot be copied off the iPad or read while the iPad is locked. The app sends an APNs device token and its update credential to the same service, and nothing else.

When the organization posts a notice, the push sent to the iPad app through APNs carries the short headline of that notice, signed by the organization. The app checks that signature before it shows anything. Apple's service carries that headline. Nothing else about your card goes through Apple.

Text messages

What we collect. To send you Response Card text messages we keep your mobile phone number and a record that you agreed to receive the messages. Nothing else about you is collected for the text message program.

How you opt in. You give your mobile number to SpunkyChicken, or to the organization that issued your card, and agree to receive Response Card text messages at that number. Agreeing is not a condition of any purchase.

How we use your number. Only to send the service messages you agreed to: a link to install your card or an update to it, a request to confirm that your device is with you, and a set-up code for adding your card to a tablet. We do not send marketing or promotional messages. Message frequency varies, typically fewer than five a month. Message and data rates may apply.

Opting out. Reply STOP to any message to stop receiving them; you will get one message confirming it. Reply HELP for help.

Sharing. No mobile information will be shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing or promotional purposes. Information sharing to subcontractors in support services, such as customer service, is permitted. All other use case categories exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent; this information will not be shared with any third parties.

Messages are carried by our messaging provider, Twilio, solely to deliver them. Each message's record, including its text and any picture, is deleted from Twilio once it has been delivered or has failed.

What the app does not do

  • The app itself does not collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts. Your phone number is held only for the text message program described above, never by the app.
  • It does not track you, profile you, or show advertising.
  • Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared with third parties except the service providers named in this policy (Google and Apple for notifications, Apple for Wallet, Twilio for text messages), and only to deliver what is described here.

Notifications

On Android, notifications are delivered through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, which receives a token identifying your installation in order to route the message.

When the organization posts a notice, the short headline of that notice is sent this way so your phone can show it without being unlocked. Google's service carries that headline. It is written by the organization for exactly this, and it is the same line your lock screen shows.

Nothing else about your card is sent this way. The full notice, your numbers and your login stay encrypted on your device and are never sent to Google.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored on the device. On iPhone, removing the pass from Wallet deletes it from the iPhone and stops its updates. To have the card itself withdrawn, or your mobile number removed from the text message program, contact the organization that issued it. Replying STOP also ends all text messages.

Contact

Response Card is operated by SpunkyChicken. For questions about this policy, contact the organization that issued your card, or reply HELP to any Response Card text message.
