Text messages

SpunkyChicken sends text messages (SMS and MMS) about your card, on behalf of the organization that issued it. The SpunkyChicken SMS terms cover the program in full.

What we collect

Your mobile phone number, and a record that you agreed to receive SpunkyChicken text messages at that number. Nothing else about you is collected for the text message program.

How you opt in

You give your mobile number to the organization issuing your card, or to SpunkyChicken, and agree to receive SpunkyChicken text messages about your card at that number. Messages are sent only after you agree. Agreeing is not a condition of any purchase.

How we use your number

Only to send the service messages you agreed to:

  • a link to add your card, or an update to it;
  • a request to confirm that your device is still with you;
  • a set-up code, sent as a picture message, for adding your card to a tablet.

We do not send marketing or promotional messages. Message frequency varies, typically fewer than five a month. Message and data rates may apply.

Opting out

Reply STOP to any message to stop receiving them. You will get one message confirming it, and nothing after that. Reply HELP for help.

Who carries the messages

Messages are carried by our messaging provider, Twilio, solely to deliver them. Twilio receives your number and the content of each message for that purpose. Each message's record, including its text and any picture, is deleted from Twilio once it has been delivered or has failed.

Apple Wallet card iPhone

On iPhone your card is an Apple Wallet pass. There is no app to install. The pass is signed by the organization that issued it, and Apple Wallet keeps it on your iPhone, protected by your iPhone's passcode, Face ID or Touch ID. If your Wallet passes sync through iCloud, Apple keeps the pass on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, under Apple's own privacy policy.

What Wallet sends

When you add the pass, Wallet registers it with the card service run for the organization that issued your card, so the card can be kept up to date. Wallet sends:

  • an identifier Wallet makes up for your device, and a push token from Apple Push Notification service;
  • the card's serial number and its update credential, when it asks for an update;
  • Wallet's own error messages about the pass, if something goes wrong fetching it.

The service also keeps the time and country of the card's first device registration with the card's record, so that a card used on more devices than it was issued to can be noticed.

Removing the pass

Removing the pass from Wallet deletes it from your iPhone and tells the service to stop sending updates to that device.

Apple Push Notification service

Updates reach Apple devices through Apple Push Notification service (APNs), not by text message. APNs receives a push token that identifies the device, so it can route the push. Apple handles that token under Apple's own privacy policy.

  • Wallet pass. The push carries nothing: no card contents, no name, no notice. It only tells Wallet to fetch the new card from the service over HTTPS. Wallet may then show the short headline of a notice from your organization on your lock screen.
  • iPad app. When your organization posts a notice, the push carries that notice's short headline, signed by the organization. The app checks the signature before it shows anything.

That headline is the only part of your card's content that passes through Apple. Your numbers, your login and the full notice never do.

iPad app

An iPad cannot hold a Wallet pass, so on iPad your card is held in the Secure Contact Card app. The app keeps your card encrypted with a key in the iPad's Secure Enclave. It opens only with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and it cannot be copied off the iPad or read while the iPad is locked.

The app sends an APNs push token and its update credential to the card service, and nothing else.

Android app

What the app stores on your device

  • The contact card you were issued, encrypted. It is unlocked with your fingerprint, face or device PIN, and cannot be read without one.
  • A login value, encrypted separately, shown only after you confirm again.
  • A credential that lets the app ask for updates to your card. This one is not behind your fingerprint, because the app has to use it when an update arrives and you are not there. It is unreadable while your phone is locked.

All three are encrypted with keys held in your phone's secure hardware. They cannot be copied off the device, and none of them can be read while the screen is locked. The app needs a screen lock to work; without one it says so rather than store your card.

What the app sends

  • A notification token from Google Firebase Cloud Messaging, so your card can be told when it changes.
  • The update credential above, when it asks for an update.

Both go only to the card service, over HTTPS. When your organization posts a notice, its short headline is delivered through Firebase Cloud Messaging so your phone can show it without being unlocked. Nothing else about your card is sent to Google.

What the service records

When a phone, tablet or Wallet asks for an update, the service notes the address it connected from, along with what was asked for and when. Those records are deleted after ninety days.

They exist so that a card used on more devices than it was issued to can be noticed. They are not used to work out where you are, they are not combined with anything else, and they go nowhere else.

Sharing

We do not sell your information, and we do not track you, profile you or show advertising. The app does not collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts. Your phone number is held only for the text message program, never by the app.

Information is handled only by the service providers below, and only to deliver what this policy describes:

  • Twilio: delivers text and picture messages.
  • Apple: Apple Wallet, iCloud sync of Wallet passes, and Apple Push Notification service.
  • Google: Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android.

No mobile information will be shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing or promotional purposes. Information sharing to subcontractors in support services, such as customer service, is permitted. All the above categories exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent; this information will not be shared with any third parties.

Security

Your card is encrypted on your device with keys held in its secure hardware, and every request between your device and the card service uses HTTPS. Wallet passes are signed, so a pass that has been altered will not be accepted.

Your choices

  • Stop text messages: reply STOP to any SpunkyChicken text message.
  • Remove the card from a device: uninstall the app, or remove the pass from Wallet. Either removes everything stored on that device and stops its updates.
  • Withdraw the card or remove your number: contact the organization that issued your card, or reply HELP to any SpunkyChicken text message.

Children

Secure Contact Card and SpunkyChicken text messages are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, this page is updated and the date at the top changes with it.
